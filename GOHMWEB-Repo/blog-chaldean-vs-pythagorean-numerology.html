Chaldean vs Pythagorean Numerology: Which System Is Right for You?

Numerology By GreatOhm · Updated 2026-10-10

If you have ever used two different numerology calculators on the same name and gotten two different answers, you have already met the great split in numerology: the Chaldean and Pythagorean systems. Both convert letters into numbers, but they do it in very different ways — and each has a loyal following for good reasons.

Where each system comes from

Pythagorean numerology is the more widely used system in the West. It is named after the Greek philosopher Pythagoras (c. 570–495 BCE), who is credited with proving that reality can be expressed through numbers. Its alphabet chart runs in a clean, logical order from 1 to 9, which makes it intuitive and easy to learn.

Chaldean numerology is older and comes from the ancient Babylonian region of Chaldea (roughly modern-day Iraq). It is considered by many practitioners to be closer to Vedic and mystical traditions. Its letter values are not sequential, and it treats the number 9 as sacred and therefore rarely assigned to letters.

How the letter values differ

The two systems assign different numbers to the same letters, which is why the same name produces different results. Here is the difference at a glance:

  • Pythagorean: A=1, B=2, C=3, D=4, E=5, F=6, G=7, H=8, I=9, then the cycle repeats (J=1, K=2, and so on). All 26 letters map cleanly onto 1–9.
  • Chaldean: The values follow no simple order — for example A, I, J, Q and Y all equal 1. The number 9 is treated as holy and is not given to any letter.

Which name do you use?

There is one more important difference: the Pythagorean system works well with your full birth name as it appears on your birth certificate. The Chaldean system traditionally focuses on the name you are known by today — the name people actually call you — because Chaldean practitioners believe your current name carries your present-day vibration.

So which one should you use?

Neither system is "right" or "wrong" — they answer slightly different questions:

  • Choose Pythagorean if you want a clear, structured, easy-to-follow system that is widely documented and great for beginners. It is the standard behind most western "life path number" calculators.
  • Choose Chaldean if you are drawn to the more mystical, Vedic-leaning tradition and want an analysis tied to the name you use in daily life.

Many students of numerology learn both and compare the results, treating each as a different lens on the same person.
