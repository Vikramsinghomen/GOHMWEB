What Do Reversed Tarot Cards Mean? A Calm Beginner’s Guide

Tarot By GreatOhm · Updated 2026-10-10

One of the biggest sources of anxiety for new tarot readers is the reversed card. A card flips upside-down and a thousand questions flood in: is it bad? Is it the opposite? Did I do something wrong? Take a breath — a reversal is not bad luck. It is simply another layer of meaning, and once you understand how to read it, reversals become one of the most insightful parts of a spread.

How a card ends up reversed

Most readers shuffle without deliberately orienting the cards, so some naturally land upside-down. That randomness is fine. A reversal is not a punishment — it is a variation, the same way a word can have a softer or more internal meaning depending on how it is used.

The four main ways to read a reversal

There is no single "correct" school of thought. Here are the four most common approaches, and most readers blend them:

  • Blocked or delayed energy: the card's energy is present but slowed, internalised or meeting resistance. The Sun reversed might be confidence that is temporarily dimmed, not gone.
  • Inward or subconscious: the quality is turned inward rather than expressed outwardly. Justice reversed might be an inner sense of fairness you are wrestling with.
  • The shadow side: the less mature expression of the card — an imbalance of its theme rather than its opposite.
  • The opposite meaning: the most literal approach, treating the reversal as the direct opposite of the upright meaning. This works, but it can feel harsh and is best used alongside the other approaches.

A simple method you can use today

When a card is reversed, ask yourself two questions:

  1. What is the upright meaning? Start from the core theme of the card.
  2. How might this theme be blocked, turned inward, or not fully expressed right now? This single question resolves most reversals without drama.

For example, the Eight of Cups upright is "walking away to seek deeper meaning." Reversed, it might be hesitating to walk away, or returning to something you had left — not a disaster, just a different stage of the same journey.

Reversals are context-dependent

The same reversed card means different things in different positions. In a "what is holding me back" position, a reversal often points directly to the block. In a "what is my potential" position, it may simply describe energy that is still developing. Always read the reversal in the context of the question and its position in the spread.
